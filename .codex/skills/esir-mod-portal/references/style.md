# Writing and visuals

Use familiar words: machines, recipes, research, power, trains, enemies, settings, circuit signals. Say what a feature does and how to use it. Preserve actual names and attribution. Avoid threats, broadcast framing, exaggerated promises, performance claims without evidence, and developer jargon. Put essential meaning in selectable page text as well as media.

Examples: "Choose a receiver and send items directly to it." "Choose whether the water turret targets enemies or puts out fires first." "Reduce wasted shots by accounting for damage already on its way."

Use `portal/style.json`: 1280 x 720, charcoal, pale text and amber accents. Actual game art supplies color. Keep title placement consistent and no more than two explanatory callouts at once. Use a dark editor tile background by default. Add real terrain only where the demonstrated mechanic needs it, such as the vat's wetland basin. Explicitly disable entity and decorative autoplace; clear any pre-existing decorations. Use compact working setups, steady cameras and deliberate cuts. Enlarge relevant native GUI areas instead of shrinking whole windows. A larger source viewport is appropriate when native panels would otherwise be clipped. Label different endpoints, vehicles and comparison settings.

Default to 10–20 seconds. Keep combat at normal speed. Derive time-lapse speed from real source ticks and label it. Reading holds do not imply live motion. Produce a poster and contact sheet. Review at 800 and 390 CSS pixels wide, with adjacent text carrying meaning on small screens.

Template families: GUI/action/result; combat/supply/target/result; process/stages; network/endpoints/status; production/material flow; upgrades/before/after; options/matched comparison; environment/cause/change.

Report per-file and page-wide bytes. Optimize static backgrounds and redundant frames before removing detail. An old Discord teaser's 10 MB ceiling is not a Portal limit. Never impose an invented platform limit.
